# Is early-stage (angel/seed) funding in NZ hard to find and access, and is discovery the gap?

## Executive answer

Yes, with a nuance. NZ's startup capital problem in 2025–26 is concentrated at the
**entry point**, not the total: 2025 set an all-time record for startup investment
(NZ$754m, up 61%), yet only 47 genuinely new companies received investment — below
the historical norm of 50+ — and angel capital was nearly flat while being spread
across 34% more deals. Official (MBIE) and industry (Upstart Nation) sources
independently describe a seed-market regression and an ecosystem that is "noisy,
fragmented, and disconnected", where founders struggle to find support. No
maintained, NZ-focused, machine-readable directory of early-stage funders exists;
the discovery tooling founders find online targets the US. What we could **not**
verify is a survey that directly quantifies *discovery* (as opposed to capital
scarcity) as the binding constraint — that's the key follow-up question.
**Confidence: Medium-High** (headline figures corroborated by three outlets;
several primary PDFs could not be fetched directly and are cited via press
coverage and search content).

## Evidence

**Record totals, shrinking pipeline.** Total NZ startup investment reached $754m
across 166 deals in 2025, up 61% on 2024's $467m ([b2bnews on the Young Company
Finance Autumn 2026 report](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/nz-startup-investment-hits-754m-but-new-company-pipeline-shrinks/);
[NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/personal-finance/investment/start-up-company-investment-surged-to-754m-in-2025-after-deal-activity-jumped/5B4X67OA4FANLGYZIUCXHTDLGI/);
[NBR](https://www.nbr.co.nz/business/investment-in-startups-soared-in-2025-says-new-report/)).
**Confidence: High** (three independent outlets). But only 47 genuinely new
companies received investment in 2025 (46 in 2024), "well below the historical norm
of 50-plus"; 83% of 2025 capital went to expansion-stage companies; and only 21% of
H1 2025 deals went to genuinely new companies against a historical average of
35–36% ([b2bnews](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/nz-startup-investment-hits-754m-but-new-company-pipeline-shrinks/)).
AANZ chief executive Bridget Unsworth described investors reserving capital for
follow-ons rather than new entries. **Confidence: Medium-High.**

**Angel capital flat, spread thinner.** Total angel capital rose just 2.7% to
$13.9m in 2025 while deal count surged 34% to 167 transactions; average investment
per angel fell 8% to $12,446
([b2bnews](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/nz-startup-investment-hits-754m-but-new-company-pipeline-shrinks/);
[NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/companies/banking-finance/angel-investors-spread-bets-wider-as-deal-activity-jumps-33-in-2025/premium/CKTCVCOYPZHMXCQY7QCF4J5QQ4/);
[BusinessDesk](https://businessdesk.co.nz/article/startups/angel-investment-in-startups-is-up-but-spread-more-thinly)).
**Confidence: Medium** (underlying [Catalist NZ Angel Market Report PDF](https://cdn.catalist.co.nz/Documents/Reports/2025+Annual+New+Zealand+Angel+Market+Report.pdf)
could not be fetched; figures via press coverage).

**Official acknowledgement of a seed regression.** MBIE's 9 May 2025 briefing on
access to growth capital records "regression in the seed funding market due to
recent economic conditions", in the context of reviewing Aspire fund settings
([MBIE](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/dmsdocument/31135-access-growth-capital)).
**Confidence: Medium-High** (official document; content via search excerpt).

**Fragmented discovery is a named problem.** The Startup Advisors Council's
*Upstart Nation* report (2023) found the NZ startup ecosystem "noisy, fragmented,
and disconnected", with government support spread across multiple agencies "making
it difficult for founders to find support"; its 25 recommendations included a
national coordination body ("Accelerate Aotearoa") and multiple access-to-capital
measures ([Upstart Nation, MBIE](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/assets/upstart-nation.pdf);
[Simpson Grierson](https://www.simpsongrierson.com/insights-news/legal-updates/upstart-nation-building-a-sustainable-startup-ecosystem)).
The council has since been filed under MBIE's "previous economic development work",
and implementation was reported to face "a potential roadblock"
([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/startup-council-delivers-its-bright-ideas-but-faces-a-potential-roadblock/W2F4EFWIZBBK7DJ2SZZGNCMM7U/)).
**Confidence: High** (report exists, themes); **Medium** (specific figures, via
secondary coverage).

**The discovery surface itself churned in Dec 2025.** Callaghan Innovation — the
front door for NZ R&D grants and startup programmes — was disestablished on
1 December 2025, with programmes moved to MBIE's Innovation Services at
funds.business.govt.nz; the Ārohia grants were reported permanently closed
([Callaghan disestablishment updates](https://www.callaghaninnovation.govt.nz/about-us/disestablishment-updates/);
[funds.business.govt.nz](https://funds.business.govt.nz/);
[third-party guide](https://businessloans.org.nz/guides/innovation-services-mbie-funding/)).
**Confidence: High** (disestablishment and transfer); **Medium** (Ārohia closure —
single third-party source).

**No NZ-native discovery tooling.** Existing NZ resources are partial and not
machine-readable: What Founders Want's directories
([whatfounderswant.com](https://whatfounderswant.com/)), MoneyHub's angel guide
([moneyhub.co.nz](https://www.moneyhub.co.nz/how-to-raise-money-from-angel-investors.html)),
and Enterprise Angels' AngelEquity listing
([angelequity.co.nz](https://www.angelequity.co.nz/home/about/angel-networks/)).
The polished founder-investor matching products are overseas-focused: Metal
([metal.so](https://www.metal.so/)) and Cherub
([investwithcherub.com](https://www.investwithcherub.com/),
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/17/cherub-the-raya-of-angel-investing-is-reinventing-angel-rounds-with-a-950-subscription/)).
**Confidence: High** (existence/focus of each).

## What would change this conclusion

- The primary PDFs (YCF Autumn 2026, Catalist Angel Market Report FY25, MBIE
  briefing) becoming fetchable and contradicting the press-reported figures.
- A founder survey showing capital scarcity, not discovery, dominates failure to
  raise — that would redirect the solution from a directory toward capital-supply
  advocacy.
- Evidence of an existing maintained NZ funder dataset we missed (in which case:
  contribute, don't duplicate).

## What we couldn't verify

- Reconciliation of the 2023 series ($163.7m "early-stage investor" deals vs the
  ~$348m total implied by 2024's "+34%") — the two measures have different scopes.
- 167 angel "transactions" vs 82 "businesses" in 2025 (likely investments vs
  companies, unconfirmed).
- Any NZ survey directly quantifying discovery as the barrier (gap, not a finding).
- Current activity of some regional players (e.g. Launch Taranaki; Kōkiri's
  post-Callaghan status).

## Follow-up questions

1. What proportion of NZ founders who fail to raise cite discovery/access rather
   than lack of available capital? (Needs primary research or FMA/AANZ survey data.)
2. What is the authoritative, current list of open government funding instruments
   post-Callaghan, and who maintains it?
3. Do raise rates differ materially by region and by proximity to an angel network?
4. What are the current funding pathways for Māori and Pasifika founders, and their
   discoverability?
